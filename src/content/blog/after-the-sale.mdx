Your customer just paid.

The order confirmation went out. The team celebrated. Then everyone moved on to the next click.

That is the moment most consumer brands lose the plot.

The first purchase is not the finish line. It is the starting gun for the revenue that compounds: the second order, the refill, the rebooking, the friend they send your way.

This used to be **The Expansion Signal**. It is now **Revenue After The Sale**, the Saasible newsletter for what happens after checkout. Same writer. Clearer promise. For D2C founders, brand marketers, clinics, gyms and consumer brands that sell to people, not to other software companies.

## Who this is for

If you paid to win a customer and then went quiet, this is for you.

If your WhatsApp, SMS or email only speaks when there is a discount to push, this is for you.

If your clinic or gym fills the calendar with new enquiries while past clients drift away, this is for you.

## The leak after the sale

Money goes into ads, landing pages and checkout. The purchase lands. Then the journey stops.

No onboarding that helps the customer get value in the first week. No nudge when the product should be running low. No check in after a service visit. No win back when someone goes silent.

The brand paid for attention once. The customer was ready to hear more. Then WhatsApp, SMS and email all went cold.

That silence is expensive. Not because of one dramatic churn event. Because of a hundred small absences: the refill with no reminder, the booking with no follow up, the buyer who forgot why they chose you.

You do not need a bigger ad budget. You need a clearer map of where the relationship leaks after payment.

## A five question leak check

Answer these for one product line, one clinic service or one membership. Be honest. A yes is a leak.

1. **After payment, what happens in the first 48 hours besides the receipt?**  
   If the only message is transactional, the customer has no reason to feel looked after.

2. **Do you know the natural moment for a second purchase or rebooking?**  
   Days until a refill, weeks until a follow up visit, months until a season change. If you cannot name that window, you cannot message inside it.

3. **When a buyer goes quiet for 30 days, does anyone notice?**  
   Silence is data. If nobody sees it, nobody acts.

4. **Which channel does this customer already open?**  
   WhatsApp in many markets. SMS or email in others. Voice for some service businesses. If your follow up lives on a channel they ignore, the journey is decorative.

5. **Is your next message useful, or only promotional?**  
   A tip, a how to use note, or a reminder timed to real usage beats another blast about a sale.

One yes is a weak spot. Three or more means the post purchase system is mostly hope.

Hypothetical: a skincare brand ships a perfect first order, then sends nothing for six weeks except a festival discount. The customer finishes the bottle in four weeks, buys a neighbour’s brand in week five, and opens the discount as a stranger in week six. That is not a loyalty problem. That is a calendar problem.

## One action this week

Pick one journey. Keep it small.

**Write a three message sequence for the first 14 days after purchase.**

- **Message 1 (within 24 hours):** thank them, confirm what happens next, and give one tip that helps them use what they bought. Send it on the channel they already used to buy or book.
- **Message 2 (around day 5 to 7):** ask one simple question about how it is going, or share the most common mistake new customers make. Invite a reply. A reply is a relationship.
- **Message 3 (around day 12 to 14):** point to the natural next step. A refill window. A rebooking link. A complementary product. A membership reminder. Timed to use, not to your campaign calendar.

Rules for this week: no stack of discounts, no link shortener tricks that get WhatsApp templates rejected, no robot sounding copy, and one clear next step per message.

If you only have email today, start there. If customers live on WhatsApp, draft the same three beats and check Meta template rules before you submit.

Three messages. Fourteen days. One product or service. Enough to learn whether silence was strategy or just the default.
